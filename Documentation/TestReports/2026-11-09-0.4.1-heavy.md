## Findings

1. **No frame-time problem even in endgame Calamity fights on this machine.** All runs hold 60 FPS; one frame above
   25 ms in 21 500 frames; no hitches (frames > 2× median) at all.
2. **Calamity's player update scales with the number of active NPCs:** across 8 Calamity runs (2 launches, idle to 89 NPCs)
   `update_players ≈ 0.257 ms + 2.42 µs × active NPCs` (R² = 0.98). Correlation, not a proof of cause: SCal also has many
   projectiles, but zombies/frost moon (no projectiles) fit the same line. Vanilla player update also iterates NPCs
   (collision), and only one vanilla data point exists (0.069 ms at ~5 NPCs), so the vanilla part of the slope is unknown.
3. **Per-NPC update cost with Calamity:** 50 zombies add ≈ 0.35 ms to NPC update (≈ 7 µs per zombie) and ≈ 0.12 ms to
   player update. Without a vanilla 50-zombie run, Calamity's share cannot be separated.
4. **Projectiles are the largest single phase in projectile-heavy fights** (SCal 0.55–0.61 ms, Providence 0.34 ms per update).
5. **GC is the largest cost that grows with fight intensity:** SCal allocates ~25 MB/s → 1.5 gen0 collections per second,
   ~56 ms pause per minute (≈ 0.6 ms per collection on average). Still no hitch was recorded.
6. Providence shows the largest work outside the entity phases (0.13 ms per update).

## Implications for TPO

- On this hardware there is still no frame-time bottleneck that a TPO change could remove; the measured costs are inside
  Calamity's own code. CLAUDE.md/Plan 02 rules exclude patching Calamity without strong evidence and approval.
- The data supports two useful directions:
  a) repeat the heavy scenes on a **slower CPU** (or with the Windows "maximum processor state" limited), where 7.8 ms
     could become > 16.7 ms;
  b) a **vanilla 50-zombie run** (fresh launch, TPO only) to separate Calamity's per-NPC cost from vanilla's — data that
     could be reported upstream.
